Age Twisters, the latest title from **Piccolo Studios in Barcelona**, proves its whimsical nature is more than skin-deep. Highlighted at Gamescom, this engaging co-op puzzler gives players control of a grandfather and granddaughter who receive mysterious bracelets allowing them to **'twist' their ages at will**. Swapping between child, teenager, adult, or elder forms, both characters unlock unique abilities to solve accessible and playful puzzles.

## Creative Puzzles and Generational Humor

In its hands-on demo, Age Twisters abandons grim themes for a lighthearted, family-friendly vibe. The game’s central mechanic—the ability to switch ages—directly impacts how players navigate each challenge. For instance, children can swap places with chickens (a recurring motif in one factory-themed level), while older forms limp and struggle with mobility, making certain obstacles impassable. The solution to many puzzles lies in breezy experimentation and cooperation, ensuring the game is easy for everyone to pick up.

Dialogue reflects the character’s current age: kids joke around, adults dispense wisdom, and elders grumble in hilarious fashion. Even mid-conversation, changing your character’s age alters what they say, adding a fun touch of improvisation to every moment. Failure states are just as playful—getting hit by enemies or hazards can de-age you into a helpless baby (requiring revival) or age you into a skeleton (which, humorously, also needs another player’s help to recover).

## A Game for Every Generation

Piccolo Studios’ founders say they wanted to create a game that brings generations together, departing from the darker tones of their previous releases. Age Twisters isn’t just slapstick and silliness—its core is about bridging the generational gap, using comedy and accessibility. Everything from the game’s vivid art to its parade of goofy enemies leans into this spirit, with vibrant visuals and light-hearted interactions setting the tone. 

As families are spending more time living together, Age Twisters could be a timely antidote to generational stereotypes—and just a good excuse for every age to play together.
