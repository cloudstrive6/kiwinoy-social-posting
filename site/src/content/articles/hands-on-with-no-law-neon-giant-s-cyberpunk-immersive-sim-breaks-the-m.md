Sometimes a game clicks instantly, and at Gamescom, **No Law**—the new cyberpunk FPS from Swedish developer **Neon Giant**—did just that. While cyberpunk settings are far from new, No Law promises to shake up the formula, combining smart, immersive sim design with a world that stands out both visually and mechanically. Players step into the shoes of **Grey Harkner**, a retired military man forced back into action, exploring the mysteries and power struggles unfolding in the city of Port Desire.

## A Cyberpunk City Like No Other

No Law’s world, **Port Desire**, reimagines the typical cyberpunk backdrop. Rather than the relentless corporate monoliths and endless neon often inspired by American takes, Port Desire feels distinctly European. Expect aging brick buildings washed in faded pastel colors, where futuristic drones and tech nestle among old-world architecture. There’s genuine Mediterranean flair, reminiscent of places like Monaco or Nice, with green spaces and trees fighting back against the march of progress. It’s a vision where nature’s resilience remains visible, contrasting with other cyberpunk games that render the world cold and lifeless.

## Depth in Every Detail

No Law doesn’t just impress with its city—it’s the hands-on systems that steal the show. Whether meticulously picking up snacks in a shop (and choosing to pay for them, steal them, or even creatively use a sensor to rig a grenade), or navigating apartment buildings full of seedy characters, everything in the world feels interactive. The game’s commitment to immersion goes right down to street addresses, with real numbers and signs aiding navigation. For players seeking a truly old-school experience, a HUD-free mode even requires taking notes in a virtual notebook. The approach to stealth and progression draws comparisons to Deus Ex, but No Law appears determined to address common complaints, letting you tailor your playstyle without being boxed in by artificial XP rewards.
