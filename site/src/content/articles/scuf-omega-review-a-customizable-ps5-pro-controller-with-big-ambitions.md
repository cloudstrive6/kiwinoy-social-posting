**The SCUF Omega enters the market as an officially licensed pro controller for both PlayStation 5 and PC, offering a highly customizable design for competitive players willing to invest in premium hardware.** With support for both wired and wireless (via dongle or Bluetooth) connectivity, the Omega promises flexibility along with its bold looks—if you’re ready for a starting price of $219.99/£209.99, and up to $229.99/£229.99 depending on personal options.

## Customization and Features

Unboxing the Omega is a straightforward affair, with a PlayStation-branded box revealing a sturdy hard case, the controller, wireless dongle, and a braided USB-A to C cable. **Customization is clearly a highlight here**, with SCUF offering a range of faceplates, button colors, thumbstick types, and even custom touchpad designs via its website. Some choices will bump up the price further, making this a controller aimed at those who care about both aesthetics and performance. The review unit featured a Cherry Blossom design, showing off just one of many options available.

In terms of ergonomics, the Omega is light without feeling cheap and features comfortable rubber grips. The face buttons provide a satisfying, clicky response reminiscent of a gaming mouse, and similar feedback can be applied to the triggers with a physical switch. Notably, the controller relies on TMR (Tunnelling Magnetoresistance) thumbsticks for improved accuracy and reduced risk of stick drift compared to Hall Effect alternatives. Extra buttons include four rear paddles, side buttons, and a cluster of programmable controls for volume and mic at the bottom. However, the Omega omits both rumble and adaptive triggers.

## Real-World Play and Drawbacks

Connecting and programming the Omega is simple, whether on PS5 or PC, though physically toggling a switch under the removable magnetic faceplate is required to change from dongle to Bluetooth or between platforms—a minor but notable hurdle. SCUF’s app streamlines button remapping and lets you save up to three user profiles, ideal for quickly switching between different games and playstyles.

In gameplay, the controller impresses with its tactile feedback and fast response—especially in FPS titles—but not every addition stands out. The side and rear buttons can be awkward, and for users with larger hands, the Omega’s relatively compact size might lead to uncomfortable stretching to reach all inputs.
