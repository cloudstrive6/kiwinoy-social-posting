Few X-Men characters have captured imaginations quite like **Wolverine**, and Marvel’s newest miniseries, **Wolverine: Paradise #1**, sends Logan on a quest for peace in an unlikely locale—Florida. Ditching the superhero life, this series follows Wolverine as he tries to escape his troubled past and settle down in Miami. But as ever, trouble finds him, and he’s soon swept up into a string of conflicts that draw in familiar faces from the Marvel universe.

## A Sun-Soaked Setup that Lacks Bite

Set some time after **Jean Grey’s first death** and Logan’s departure from the X-Mansion, Wolverine: Paradise #1 opens with the hero leaving the team behind, seeking a new beginning. On the road, he encounters a mysterious old man connected to his past, and upon arriving in Miami, bumps into mutant celebrity **Dazzler**—who seeks his help against Hammerhead. Despite his intentions to lay low, Wolverine inevitably ends up drawn into a fresh round of action, while new characters with surprising abilities—such as fast healing and claws reminiscent of Sabertooth—enter the fray. While this all sounds like classic Wolverine stuff on paper, the issue falters with too much dense exposition and narration that explains more than it shows, leaving the story feeling hollow and leaving little room for emotional resonance.

## Gritty Art Anchors an Uneven Read

**Dan Panosian** handles writing, art, and colors, delivering visuals that suit Wolverine’s rugged persona. The rough, textured artwork and expressive character designs evoke the spirit of classic Wolverine tales, though at times, the fight scenes suffer from jarring perspective shifts and panel overload. Notably, **Cory Petit’s lettering** stands out with lively sound effects that enhance the action, bringing personality to everything from Dazzler’s speakers to gunfire. While Wolverine’s vacation look initially divides opinion, it ultimately fits the story’s Miami vibe and Logan’s attempt at relaxation. However, despite strong visuals, the lack of narrative focus and emotional depth keeps this debut from reaching its full potential.
