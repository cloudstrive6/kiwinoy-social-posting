A browser-based clone of **Old School RuneScape** called Elderwood, developed using GPT-6 Astra by former OpenAI employee Rohan Varma, has been pulled offline just days after it appeared. The game, which closely mirrored the look and feel of RuneScape, sparked swift backlash from the community and quickly drew the attention of Jagex, RuneScape’s developer, who issued a takedown notice.

## How Elderwood Emerged and Fell
Elderwood made its debut this week, offering players a familiar multiplayer adventure experience directly in their browsers. In a promotional video generated by AI, Elderwood showcased gameplay nearly identical to RuneScape—complete with character spawning outside Lumbridge castle, skilling, and combat—though a few inconsistencies stood out. As Rohan Varma publicly shared updates and continued to expand the project, including adding areas like Varrock and Falador, it provoked a surge of criticism on platforms like Reddit and X. RuneScape fans quickly called out Elderwood for using shamelessly borrowed art assets and lamented the rise of "AI bros," while user sentiment ranged from mockery to outright frustration.

## Community Criticism and Quick Shutdown
Following the heightened attention, the number of players visiting Elderwood spiked, prompting Varma to implement content moderation updates. However, it wasn't enough to stave off action from Jagex, who issued a formal takedown request. The Elderwood website now hosts a farewell message thanking its short-lived community and leaving high scores as a memento, but players continued to criticize the project’s handling and tone even in its final statement. The incident highlights broader tensions between generative AI and traditional game development in the RuneScape community—where botting and asset copying have already created long-standing issues.

For those seeking the authentic RuneScape experience, Jagex recently added two new quests to Old School RuneScape, underscoring the enduring appeal of the original.
