Magic: The Gathering’s **Foundations Commander decks are back in stock at major retailers like Amazon** following weeks of tight supply since their **October 2, 2026** debut alongside the Reality Fracture set. Aimed at new and returning players, these five preconstructed decks offer affordable, easy-to-play entry points into Commander, the game’s most popular multiplayer format. Each deck features a mono-color, creature-themed strategy—think Angels, Dragons, Dinosaurs, Zombies, or artifact synergies.

## Availability and Price Trends
While the **MSRP is $30 per deck**, some have crept above that due to ongoing demand and limited availability since launch. For instance, **Reign of Dragons**, the mono-red deck, is listed at $35.44 on Amazon, notably undercutting its current TCGplayer average. Meanwhile, the mono-black zombie deck, **Wretched Ranks**, sits at $39.99, closely matching its market rate. Artifact-focused **Keen Engineering** is priced higher on Amazon at $43.07, but has recently been under MSRP and could drop again. The standout deal? **Calling All Angels** is back to MSRP at $29.99—over $22 less than its TCGplayer average. The five-deck bundle now hovers around $195, a significant hike over the combined $149.95 MSRP. As for **Tramplesaurus Rex**, the green dinosaur deck, it’s still out of stock on major platforms and currently commands a hefty $60 in the secondary market—double its list price.

## What to Expect from Foundations Decks
The Foundations series lives up to its name by providing straightforward, cohesive gameplay for both newcomers and seasoned players introducing friends to Commander. Each deck revolves around a classic creature type, led by popular commanders like Giada, Font of Hope (Angels), Lathliss, Dragon Queen (Dragons), Ghalta, Primal Hunger (Dinosaurs), Ghoulcaller Gisa (Zombies), and Sai, Master Thopterist (Artifacts). Wizards of the Coast is expected to keep these decks in print, which should eventually temper rising prices—especially for the harder-to-find green deck. For now, buying individual decks closer to MSRP offers the best value, and patient players may want to wait for future restocks rather than pay a premium.
