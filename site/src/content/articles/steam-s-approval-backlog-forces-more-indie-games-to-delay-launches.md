More indie developers say they've been forced to delay upcoming releases because of **Steam's growing approvals backlog**, a relatively recent problem that's believed to stem from an influx of AI slop on the platform. Despite Valve's recent attempts to tighten submission rules, one week after the issue first surfaced the situation doesn't appear to have improved.

## Games caught in the queue

Earlier this month, developers began complaining that delays in Valve's approval process were extending the time it took to publish their games. Valve responded that approval times could now take **up to 12 days**, due to the number of submissions it was having to re-review.

One affected title is the turn-based tower defence game **Australia Did It: End-of-the-World Railway Defense Business**, co-developed by indie designer **Rami Ismail** and Aesthetician Labs. It was set for release on **10th November** but has been delayed. Publisher Mystic Forge wrote on the Steam forums that the team has been stuck waiting for a few weeks and doesn't know when Valve will get around to reviewing the latest build.

Ismail told Eurogamer the game has been in the queue since **20th October**. It failed over a minor controller input issue on 5th November, which the team resolved the same day, and they've been waiting since with no meaningful response from Valve despite follow-ups. He said 10-14 days pre-launch used to be enough to fail twice and still ship on time.

**Amazing Slot Survivor (A.S.S.)**, the debut title from TwoMenGames, has also retracted its release date. The game, which puts a slot machine spin on the bullet heaven auto-shooting genre, was set for **13th November**. Its demo, previously due to disappear at full release, has returned to Steam until a new launch date is announced. Social media suggests others, including Bottled by Bears and Food Devils, are affected too.

## Is Valve's fix enough?

Valve recently introduced stricter submission guidelines meant to keep the lowest-effort games, such as those vibe-coded entirely with gen-AI, out of its approval queue. Ismail doesn't think that goes far enough.

He said he is concerned that Valve avoiding a political position on AI "is forcing them into choices that will inevitably be worse for developers." If automated systems let people submit several game clones a day, he argued, Valve's only options are to constrain distribution, increase fees or complicate the process, and none of those outcomes lets Valve stay neutral on AI without making life worse for genuine developers.
