With **Avengers: Doomsday** on the horizon, Marvel fans are buzzing about the movie’s production—and whether it can live up to sky-high expectations. The project has recently come into the spotlight again following reports of **late-game reshoots**, as directors **Joe and Anthony Russo** begin shedding light on the creative vision behind the highly anticipated film.

## Sergio Leone Inspires the Marvel Multiverse

In a new interview, the Russo brothers revealed that their inspiration for Avengers: Doomsday came from an unlikely place: **Sergio Leone’s Once Upon a Time in the West**. The 1968 spaghetti Western is renowned for its intricate plot and larger-than-life characters, set against an atmosphere of betrayal, rivalry, and vengeance. According to the Russos, the Western genre’s reputation for complex showdowns played a significant role in shaping their approach. They noted that, much like classic Westerns, Avengers: Doomsday will center on intense conflicts and intersecting motivations—all set within the vast Marvel universe.

## What This Means for Avengers: Doomsday

The choice to draw from Once Upon a Time in the West is especially intriguing given the Western’s focus on **rival factions, personal vendettas, and high-stakes territory battles**. While the directors’ earlier comparisons for previous films have leaned into unexpected genres, their choices have typically paid off with layered storytelling. Here, the suggestion is that Avengers: Doomsday could feature a similarly complex interplay between heroes (and possibly villains), each maneuvering for influence in the multiverse. This storytelling lens may signal a departure from the more straightforward narratives of past Avengers outings, and fans are watching closely to see how these influences reshape the action and character arcs.

**Avengers: Doomsday** is set to arrive in theaters on **December 18th**.
