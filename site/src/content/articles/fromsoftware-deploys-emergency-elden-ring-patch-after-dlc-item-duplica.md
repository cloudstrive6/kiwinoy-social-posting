Elden Ring is back in the spotlight with the arrival of its **Tarnished Edition on Switch 2**—and a surprise security scramble soon after. Alongside the debut of the long-anticipated Nintendo port, FromSoftware released a DLC pack on other platforms, bringing special content previously exclusive to the new Switch edition. But it didn’t take long for players to uncover a duplication exploit, giving rise to a wave of free DLC item sharing—even to those who hadn't purchased the add-on.

## Emergency Patch Closes the Gap
FromSoftware responded swiftly by rolling out **Patch 1.17.1 on Tuesday, September 8th across all platforms**, directly targeting the item duplication loophole. According to the official announcement, the update "fixes an issue in multiplayer where, under specific conditions, DLC-specific items could be obtained when not normally eligible." No additional content or tweaks came with the patch—it exists solely to secure the integrity of item sharing.

The affected content, available as a **$5 / £4 Tarnished Pack**, includes two new starting classes (Idus Knight and Heavy Knight), fresh armor sets, new weapons, and unique skins for the ever-loyal horse mount, Torrent. Prior to the update, players could invite a DLC owner to their game, have them drop these exclusive items, and freely pick them up—without the original player losing anything. This essentially allowed for unlimited duplication and distribution of paid content.

## Trading Remains, Duplication Gone
While the patch puts an end to the exploit, **item trading itself is still possible** in Elden Ring. Players can continue to exchange weapons, armor, and other loot, including DLC exclusives. The crucial difference now: traded items will be correctly removed from the original player's inventory, preserving fairness across the community.

First impressions of Elden Ring running on Switch 2 suggest a solid, if not perfect, port—a technical achievement that comes with its own set of challenges for FromSoftware.
