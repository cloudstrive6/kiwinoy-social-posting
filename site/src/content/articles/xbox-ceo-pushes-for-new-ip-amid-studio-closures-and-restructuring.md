Xbox is in the middle of a major shakeup, as **CEO Asha Sharma emphasized the urgent need for new, enduring IP to fuel the company's future**. Sharma’s comments, reportedly shared at an internal town hall, touched on ambitions to create global franchises and attract new audiences. But these remarks arrive on the heels of significant layoffs, studio closures, and a reorganization that’s moved teams like Obsidian away from their latest projects and back onto established brands like Fallout.

## A Confusing Message Amid Restructuring

The recent wave of internal changes at Xbox has seen **thousands of layoffs**, entire studios shuttered, and major shifts in the company’s project lineup. Some affected studios were recognized for crafting inventive new IPs or working on highly anticipated releases—yet were still cut. This apparent contradiction between bold talk about fresh creative risks and some of the industry's most cutthroat cuts has left many developers and fans scratching their heads. Rhetoric about "courage" and invention rings hollow when paired with actions that seem to double down on established franchises and reduce investment in experimental projects.

## Xbox’s Strategy: Play to Strengths or Risk the New?

Xbox’s leadership maintains that backing their strongest franchises remains a core part of their plan, aiming to transform great games into billion-dollar global properties. However, while the company has added upcoming titles like Hideo Kojima’s "Physint" to its new IP lineup, it did not fully finance that project itself. For many, this move underlines the tension at the heart of Xbox’s current strategy: chasing proven money-makers while saying all the right things about innovation. The resulting atmosphere has left more questions than answers for employees and fans alike as to what Xbox’s real priorities will look like moving forward.
