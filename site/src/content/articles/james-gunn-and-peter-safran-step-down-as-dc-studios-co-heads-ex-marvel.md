**James Gunn** and **Peter Safran** are stepping down as the co-heads of **DC Studios**, marking one of the first major executive shakeups since Warner Bros. was acquired by **Skydance**. Taking over the position will be **Jeremy Latcham**, a former executive at Marvel.

The change lands at a pivotal moment for DC's film and TV ambitions, and it brings a familiar name from the rival Marvel side into the driver's seat.

## What Gunn and Safran are still doing

Gunn is not walking away from DC entirely. He will reportedly still complete work on and release his Superman sequel, **Man of Tomorrow**, and will be involved with the spinoff series **The People v. Gorilla Grodd**. Both Gunn and Safran will also remain attached as producers on Matt Reeves' **The Batman: Part II**.

That means several of the most high-profile projects on DC's slate will keep their existing creative ties, even as the leadership structure changes.

## What Skydance is saying

Skydance chairman and CEO **David Ellison** and co-CEO **Ynon Kreiz** praised the outgoing duo in a joint statement. "James and Peter launched a new era for DC Studios and led it through a period of real transformation, resetting the foundation and building a slate we're incredibly proud of," they said. "We're grateful for their passion, contributions and commitment to this legendary brand."

The statement also addressed the handover to Latcham, describing him as a longtime creative partner to both Gunn and Safran. "Jeremy has been a longtime creative partner to both, and we have complete confidence that James and Peter will ensure a smooth, collaborative transition and continue this work with care," it continues. "We look forward to working with Jeremy to build the next generation of DC storytelling and to doubling down on our commitment to fans."

## The big picture

With Latcham taking charge, DC Studios will be led by an executive with a Marvel background, while Gunn and Safran's continued involvement on key projects points to an effort at continuity. Further details are expected to follow.
