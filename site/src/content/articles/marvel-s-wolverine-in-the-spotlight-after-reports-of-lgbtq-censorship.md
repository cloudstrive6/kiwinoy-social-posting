**Some Marvel’s Wolverine players have reported that the game’s depiction of Mystique’s lesbian relationship and marriage to Tyger has been censored in specific regional editions.** Evidence shared across Reddit and social media highlights changes to dialogue and in-game assets, with many pointing to the Middle Eastern version for these modifications.

## What Players Are Seeing

Screenshots circulating online show that a framed wedding photo of Mystique and Tyger is **blurred and left blank** in the affected versions, even though the item is still accessible by players. Players have also captured images of a letter between the couple, which appears completely obscured. Additionally, one Reddit post includes a gameplay clip where the dialogue is **muted whenever the word 'wife' is mentioned**, and subtitles for the term are replaced with asterisks. This pattern matches tactics commonly used for releasing games, films, and TV shows in regions with stricter media guidelines, where references to same-sex relationships are frequently censored.

## A Consistent Pattern in the Region

While **Mystique’s relationship is not totally removed from Marvel’s Wolverine**, references are consistently censored throughout the game's script and visual content. Fans note that the marriage between Mystique and Tyger is important to Mystique’s storyline and her dynamic with Wolverine, so the censorship stands out. This practice isn’t new for Sony and Insomniac Games — **Marvel’s Spider-Man 2 was previously altered for regional release in Saudi Arabia** to omit LGBTQ+ content, including the Pride Flag and a mission featuring a gay couple. Modifications to media in the Middle East often include muting dialogue or editing subtitles, especially when topics like religion or LGBTQ+ themes are involved.

Sony has not issued an official statement on the reported censorship in Marvel’s Wolverine. As debates continue over media modification in different regions, these changes raise questions about how games balance respecting local laws with being true to their original narratives.
