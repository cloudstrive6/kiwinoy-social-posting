**Director Zach Cregger has made significant changes to his upcoming Resident Evil movie, dialing back comedic elements after early test screenings.** Known for his unique blend of horror and humor in films like Weapons and Barbarian, Cregger’s initial cut of the survival horror adaptation leaned more into comedy than fans expected—but the feedback was immediate.

## Balancing Fear and Fun

Cregger revealed that test audiences responded positively to his Resident Evil film, but felt it was “too funny.” As a result, he chose to strip away much of the joke-heavy material. While some humor remains—a hallmark of the franchise’s beloved characters—Cregger learned that **a little goes a long way when it comes to comic relief in horror**. The director emphasized that too many laughs can undercut the tension and atmosphere that make the series so iconic, noting that occasional wisecracks work best when used sparingly.

## Staying True to Resident Evil’s Roots

The Resident Evil series is no stranger to a clever quip, especially from characters like Leon Kennedy, but franchise veterans know that horror comes first. The right dose of levity, inserted at key moments, helps viewers decompress during the tension—but overloading a horror movie with jokes can turn scares into schtick. By rebalancing the tone, Cregger found not only that audiences enjoyed the film more, but that he himself felt better about the final product.

As Cregger puts the finishing touches on Resident Evil, fans can look forward to a movie that respects both the source material and the art of balancing scares with relief.
