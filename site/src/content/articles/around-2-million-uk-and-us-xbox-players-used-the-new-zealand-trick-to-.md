Grand Theft Auto 6 has launched, and plenty of players didn't wait for their own timezone. According to data from Ampere Analysis, around **2 million UK and US players** used Xbox's so-called 'New Zealand trick' to start playing early, a figure that equals **more than a third of New Zealand's actual population**.

## How the trick works

Rockstar's regional release schedule had GTA 6 go live at **midnight on 19th November, local time, everywhere**. Because of timezones, **New Zealand was the first country in the world** to get the game.

Xbox players can make purchases on any regional store simply by changing their console's country. Impatient fans took advantage in droves, becoming temporary virtual New Zealand residents. On PlayStation the process is more finicky, since it requires a regional account and payment in local currency.

## The numbers

Ampere Analysis says the demand was significant:

- **1.5 million** Xbox users in the **US**
- **600,000** Xbox players in the **UK**
- **200,000** Xbox players in **Canada**
- **200,000** Xbox players in **Brazil**
- **100,000** Xbox players in **Mexico**

By contrast, only **100,000 PlayStation players in the US** made the early jump. Ampere points to "the ease of being able to switch country in the account settings on Xbox consoles" as the reason for the gap.

New Zealand's tourist board noticed the sudden wave of virtual visitors. It told them "We'd love to have you" and shared a video of tourists admiring the country's natural beauty, captioned "Life, because we're getting GTA 6 first."

## The big picture

The rest of the world can now officially join in. Eurogamer's Chris Tapsell gave the game five stars in his review, describing an open world of "awe, minute detail and impossible scale." GTA 6 is available on **PS5 and Xbox Series X/S**.

The numbers show how much appetite there was for the game, and how much platform account rules can shape launch-day behavior.
