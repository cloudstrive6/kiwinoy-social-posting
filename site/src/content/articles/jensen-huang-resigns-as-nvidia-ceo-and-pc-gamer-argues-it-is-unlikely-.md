**Jensen Huang** has resigned from his position as **Nvidia CEO**, ending a run as the leather-jacketed face of a company that has long been central to PC gaming hardware. The news has already drawn a split reaction from PC gamers on social media: some are shocked, some are indifferent, and some appear to be celebrating.

## Why some gamers are cheering

The celebratory response is easy to trace. Huang had become one of the most recognisable figures behind the AI boom, which is far from popular within the PC gaming hobby. At the same time, **RAM prices are ludicrously high**, with storage not far behind, and recent **graphics card price increases** have been described as mind-boggling.

Much of that is driven by demand from AI servers, and Nvidia makes the chips that power most of them, earning vast sums in the process. For some gamers, that makes Huang a symbol of Nvidia's new place in the AI economy, one that has left PC players out in the cold.

## Why his exit may not help gaming

PC Gamer's view is that Huang stepping away from the helm is **unlikely to be a good thing for PC gaming**. The numbers explain why. Gaming brought in roughly **7.4%** of Nvidia's total revenue in the **2026 fiscal year**. The company reported **$193.7 billion** in data center revenue, while its gaming division generated **$16 billion**.

That gap exists even though Nvidia is estimated to hold around **90% of the PC graphics card market**. From a pure business standpoint, the outlet notes, Nvidia would be foolish to put more focus on gaming hardware while the AI cash cow keeps delivering.

Huang, by contrast, still appeared reluctant to leave PC gaming behind. With him gone, the concern is that gaming loses its most prominent advocate inside a company whose financial priorities point firmly elsewhere.
