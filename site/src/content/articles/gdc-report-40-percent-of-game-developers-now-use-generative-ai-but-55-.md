Generative AI use among game developers is climbing, even as opposition to it grows. The latest **State of the Game Industry report from the Game Developers Conference (GDC)** estimates that as many as **40 percent** of developers active in the industry now use gen-AI tools in their work, **up four percent** on last year's report.

The report draws on responses from **more than 2,300 people**, including developers, producers, marketers, executives, and investors. Among those who say they use gen-AI, **57 percent** use it for text generation, research, early prototyping, coding, and presentations.

## Who is using it, and who isn't

The survey points to a clear divide between larger studios and smaller ones. **27 percent** of triple-A developers say they feel encouraged or pressured to use gen-AI, compared with just **nine percent** of indie developers. The tools are mostly used by business operations and services, mobile-only studios, and free-to-play developers.

Age and gender also show gaps. **54 percent** of respondents aged 45 or older report using the tools, an **eight-point increase** on last year's survey. Usage is higher among men (**45 percent**) than women (**33 percent**).

## Caution outweighs adoption

The most striking figure may be that **55 percent** of respondents think AI is having a negative impact on games. That is a larger share than the share who say they use it, which suggests caution rather than widespread adoption. The share of wary respondents has also risen **one percent** since last year, so more people now believe AI is hurting the industry than at the same point in 2025.

The source article suggests several possible reasons: sweeping layoffs that are often mentioned alongside AI, a flood of low-quality AI-made content on Steam, and some poor game launches that have clearly used gen-AI assets.

Respondents who avoid the tools were blunt about why. "I did not get into a creative industry to hand over the creativity to an algorithm," said one developer from the UK. Another, from California, said: "I got into this industry to collaborate with creative people, not to have an advanced chatbot riff on the ideas of someone who was laid off." A respondent from Minnesota added: "I believe that no one should have to read what no human has written and shouldn't have to see what no human has created."

Generative AI remains highly controversial in games. With big companies like **Epic and Microsoft** doubling down on the technology, the split between developers who want to embrace it and those who refuse looks set to keep widening.
