**Nagoshi Studio**, the indie venture from Yakuza creator **Toshihiro Nagoshi**, has officially closed its doors, ending a brief but high-profile run in the gaming world. The news comes six months after the studio’s website went dark, with final confirmation emerging from a government notice in Japan. Nagoshi, who founded the studio in 2021 after departing Sega, aimed to make a splash with a new IP—but the journey was cut short before it could properly begin.

## The Promise—and Sudden Collapse
Nagoshi Studio debuted its first game, **Gang of Dragon**, at the 2025 Game Awards with a CGI teaser featuring Ma Dong-Seok—an announcement that drew quick comparisons to Korean action cinema but offered little in terms of actual gameplay details. Hopes were high for a spiritual successor to Nagoshi’s Yakuza legacy, but these quickly faded when publishing partner NetEase pulled funding just months later. This move was part of a wider industry trend of major companies tightening support for external projects, leaving the fledgling studio without the backing it needed.

## Website Disappearance and Lost Hope
After the funding collapse, Nagoshi Studio reportedly failed to secure new investors. By May, the studio’s official website had vanished, though the Steam page for Gang of Dragon remained—fueling faint hopes among fans that a last-minute rescue might be in the cards. Now, with the government notice posted and silence from the developers, any remaining optimism has been extinguished. For those curious, the Gang of Dragon listing is still up on Steam, but the studio is no more.
