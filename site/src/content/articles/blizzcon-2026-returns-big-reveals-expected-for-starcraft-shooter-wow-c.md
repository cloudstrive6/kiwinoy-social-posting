**BlizzCon 2026** is back this weekend after a two-year hiatus, and it’s shaping up to be one of Blizzard’s most anticipated shows yet. The **opening ceremony kicks off at 6.30pm BST** with a full slate of reveals and updates expected over its hour and fifteen minute runtime. Fans around the world can find local streaming times on the official BlizzCon site, which adjusts the schedule to your region automatically.

## What to Expect on Opening Night

The headline of this year’s show is widely believed to be the **long-rumored StarCraft shooter**. Led by **former Far Cry boss Dan Hay**, this new project is rumored to be a co-op shooter, similar in style to Warhammer 40,000: Space Marine 2, instead of an always-online multiplayer shooter. There’s some speculation about who is developing it—with Nexon’s name in the mix—but fans are eager to see exactly how this project reimagines the classic StarCraft universe and whether Blizzard, known for its multiplayer roots, can successfully branch into dedicated co-op experiences.

## WoW Classic Plus and Other Announcements Likely

World of Warcraft fans should also keep an eye out for official news on the much-discussed **“Classic Plus” version of WoW**. This update aims to take the original, stripped-back WoW Classic experience in a new direction, potentially with all-new content and updates to keep long-time adventurers engaged. This follows Blizzard’s recent approach of evolving classic games, like adding a Warlock class to Diablo 2 earlier this year.

Beyond the headliners, expect reveals about new **expansions for both Overwatch and Diablo 4**, which have moved to a near-annual expansion schedule. With the last Diablo 4 expansion, Lord of Hatred, launching in April, and Overwatch’s Reign of Talon relaunch in February, fans are eager for news about what’s next for these franchises. There’s also speculation we could finally see confirmation of a **Switch 2 version of Diablo 4**.

## The Big Picture

This year’s BlizzCon comes in the wake of recent job cuts at Microsoft, Blizzard’s parent company. While Blizzard itself appears to have largely avoided significant reductions, the atmosphere might be shaped by the historic union recognition vote held just days before the event. There’s potential for protest activity as the weekend unfolds, even as fans focus on the lineup of games.
