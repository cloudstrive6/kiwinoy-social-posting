**Skyblivion**, the ambitious fan remake that brings The Elder Scrolls IV: Oblivion to life in Skyrim’s engine, is grappling with internal strife. The project, developed by volunteers under the TES Renewal Project banner, now faces a potential lawsuit after a contributor who departed the team objected to the use and maintenance of their code within the project.

## The Dispute Over Contributed Code

The disagreement erupted after one of Skyblivion’s former volunteers lost access to the project's source code repository. According to a public statement from the Skyblivion team, the group decompiled sections of the ex-volunteer’s code to address technical issues—a move they say was necessary for maintaining the project after access was revoked. The dispute escalated into legal threats from the former team member, who also demanded personal information of team leads. In response, the Skyblivion team described these demands as disproportionate and stated they could not comply.

## Project Response and Ongoing Development

Seeking to shield their team from further stress, Skyblivion’s developers have consulted legal counsel and expressed confidence about their right to use the contributed code. Nevertheless, to avoid complications, they chose to remove the disputed code and rebuild the affected system from scratch. Despite these actions, the legal threats from the former volunteer have reportedly continued, though there is no public confirmation that a lawsuit has officially been filed.

Through it all, Skyblivion’s team has asked fans not to target anyone involved and expressed their desire to resolve the situation quickly so development can proceed toward the game’s planned release. The situation follows a previous project delay and comes on the heels of Bethesda’s own Oblivion Remastered release. No further delays or changes in overall project vision have been announced.
