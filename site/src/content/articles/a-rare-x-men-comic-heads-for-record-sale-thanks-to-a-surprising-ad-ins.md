For many fans, the X-Men seem like perennial Marvel icons, but their path to superstardom was far from straightforward. **The Shi'Ar Imperial Guard made their debut in X-Men #107 (1977),** but now, decades later, this once-overlooked comic is grabbing the spotlight for reasons that have little to do with the storyline or the characters themselves.

## More Than Just a First Appearance

The Shi'Ar Imperial Guard, introduced during **Chris Claremont’s legendary sixteen-year Uncanny X-Men run**, never achieved true mainstream stardom outside of dedicated fan circles. Despite occasional appearances in **X-Men: The Animated Series, X-Men '97, and toy lines**, the group has largely remained a deep cut. But now, a CGC 9.8 graded copy of X-Men #107, featuring a rare **Mark Jewelers ad insert**, is expected to sell for over **$4,000**—a value driven by collectible scarcity rather than just mutant fever. This particular issue stands out as there are **no higher-graded copies on the CGC census**, and its last sale hit nearly **$14,000**.

## Behind the Scenes: Inspiration and Legacy

As Marvel sought powerful new adversaries for the revitalized X-Men, artist **Dave Cockrum** designed the Shi'Ar Imperial Guard as an explicit nod to DC’s Legion of Super-Heroes—a team he previously helped define. Each Guard member mirrors a Legionnaire, with their leader, **Gladiator**, drawing inspiration from Superboy/Superman—cementing Gladiator as one of Marvel’s heavy hitters. This anti-team dynamic allowed Chris Claremont to stage epic, character-driven clashes, most notably in **Uncanny X-Men #137**, where the Guard’s role was to provide dynamic action, even when their impact on plot was secondary.

The comic’s current spike in auction value reflects the ongoing effect of speculative pricing as fans anticipate the X-Men’s return to the MCU. As even "minor keys" like X-Men #107 see increased demand, it’s clear that both nostalgia and rare collecting quirks are driving the current comics market.
