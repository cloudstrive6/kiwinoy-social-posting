Real-time strategy fans, take heart: **Frank Klepacki**, the celebrated composer behind iconic games like Eye of the Beholder 2 and Command & Conquer, believes the RTS genre is far from dead. While RTS dominance has faded since its heyday in the '90s, Klepacki maintains that the genre still has plenty of life—and he’s not alone, as a fresh wave of strategy titles and expansions hit the scene.

## RTS Isn’t Going Anywhere

Klepacki, who has deep roots in the genre, challenges the doomsayers who dismissed RTS as a relic. He points out that, despite **ten years of doom-and-gloom predictions**, new strategy games and campaigns continue to launch. "If there are games coming out, the genre's not going away. It’s just that other types of games have taken the forefront," Klepacki says. Classic franchises like Warcraft, Command & Conquer, and Dune once ruled the genre, but traditional RTS gameplay is making a comeback. This year even saw **a new Warcraft 3 campaign**, alongside new titles inspired by genre staples like Age of Empires and Command & Conquer.

## The State of Strategy Today

Klepacki also highlights that the real challenge facing RTS isn't a lack of audience, but rather a shift in development priorities. He notes a "hollowing-out" of the industry, where indie studios are willing to take creative risks, while big-budget developers tend to play it safe. Yet, strategy DNA runs deep—the MOBA genre borrows heavily from RTS mechanics, and classic brands like Company of Heroes, Ruse, and Age of Mythology keep the spirit alive. Meanwhile, some studios continue experimenting with the formula, proving that RTS is evolving, not disappearing.
