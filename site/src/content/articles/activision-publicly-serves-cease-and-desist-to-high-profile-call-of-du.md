In its ongoing crusade against cheaters, **Activision has publicly served a cease-and-desist order to Elocarry**, a well-known creator of cheats for titles including Call of Duty. The move was highly visible, as the publisher posted a video to the official Call of Duty social accounts showing a legal representative delivering the order directly to Elocarry’s front door.

## Taking the Fight to Cheat Makers
Far from the dramatic confrontations of reality TV, the exchange was concise and professional, meant more as a statement than entertainment. **Activision’s clear objective is to send a public warning** not just to Elocarry, but to others manufacturing or distributing cheating software for Call of Duty and other games. By capturing and sharing the moment, Activision is demonstrating its willingness to pursue legal action transparently—and for the gaming community at large to witness.

## Disrupting the Cheating Ecosystem
Activision revealed its broader efforts alongside this event. The company shared that it has **served 80 cease-and-desist orders**, disrupted **375 cheat reseller operations**, and taken down **30 vendors** to date. These actions underscore Activision’s commitment to making life difficult for cheat makers and resellers worldwide. Following the order, **Elocarry reportedly paused sales of Call of Duty cheats**, citing a need to review the situation internally. The publisher also highlighted ongoing progress with its Ricochet Anti-Cheat tech, noting that during recent beta tests, infection rates were down and cheat evasions became harder, signaling an encouraging turn in the fight for fair play.
