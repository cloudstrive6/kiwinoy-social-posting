Sony has defeated a major **£2 billion** UK class action lawsuit over the price of games on the **PlayStation Store**. The judgment, published by London's **Competition Appeal Tribunal on 17th November**, rejected the claim that Sony had removed "all actual and potential competition" in the sale of digital games on its consoles.

The ruling follows a **30-day** tribunal hearing in London. The case was brought by **Alex Neill**, who said he represented roughly **12.2 million** gamers. It accused Sony of applying "excessive and unfair" pricing on games sold through the storefront accessible on **PS3, PS4 and PS5**.

## Why the Tribunal sided with Sony

The lawsuit argued that once you bought a PS5 or another PlayStation console, you were trapped in its ecosystem and forced to pay the prices Sony set. The Tribunal disagreed. It ruled that Sony competes within a wider "systems market" alongside **Xbox, Nintendo and PC**, and described the market definitions used to characterise Sony's conduct as "unsafe".

The judgment also said consumers "would be likely to make informed choices when purchasing consoles" and games. In its words: "Potential PlayStation console purchasers had both access to sufficient information and an incentive to consider that information in order to make informed purchasing decisions at the console level, especially in the case of high-value users who spend significant amounts in the digital gaming aftermarket."

The Tribunal also found no evidence that other digital storefronts would actually have brought PlayStation Store prices down. It noted that consumers can buy physical games instead of using the store, a situation that is changing from the **start of 2028**. That did not appear to affect the decision.

## The bigger picture

The case did not seek to examine the ownership status of digital games, which has been central to the movement opposing Sony's decision to end physical media. Earlier this year, Sony's legal team argued that "reasonable consumers" already know they don't actually own their digital games.

The case began in **2022** as an attempt to sue Sony for **£5bn** for "ripping people off" on the PlayStation Store. Sony argued the case was "flawed from start to finish".

It isn't the only legal challenge of its kind. Last year, PlayStation was sued in the **Netherlands** over the so-called "Sony tax", which highlighted artificially inflated prices on the Store. That case sought to represent more than **20,000** participants who felt short-changed.
