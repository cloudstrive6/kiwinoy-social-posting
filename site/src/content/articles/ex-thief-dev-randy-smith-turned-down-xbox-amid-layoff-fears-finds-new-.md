Veteran game designer **Randy Smith**, best known for his role in the creation of the **Thief** series, faced a pivotal moment after years of pitching a unique, nonviolent RPG concept. Describing what he calls a "lowest moment" in his search for backing, Smith was approached by a Microsoft-backed Xbox studio—just as broader industry layoffs were shaking the gaming world.

## Why Xbox Was a Hard Sell
Smith revealed his hesitation to work with Xbox stemmed from the company's history of studio closures. When Microsoft shuttered Arkane Austin, a studio with deep roots in the immersive sim genre and a commitment to creative design, it sent shockwaves through the development community. Smith, with personal connections to many at Arkane, viewed the abrupt closure and the troubled launch of Redfall as a warning sign about the risks of joining a major platform holder.

Smith described the situation as akin to "walking past some stakes with human skulls on them," signaling clear danger for teams hoping for long-term stability under Xbox. Ultimately, his choice to look elsewhere paid off, as he now leads a project with independent funding—turning a moment of near-defeat into one of renewed opportunity.

## What’s Next for Smith
Having avoided what he felt could be an uncertain future with Xbox, Smith’s new endeavor builds on the player-driven, immersive design pillars that defined his earlier work. As the gaming industry faces more shake-ups and evolving models for funding, his experience highlights the growing importance of independence for creators focused on original ideas rather than sequels or spiritual successors.
