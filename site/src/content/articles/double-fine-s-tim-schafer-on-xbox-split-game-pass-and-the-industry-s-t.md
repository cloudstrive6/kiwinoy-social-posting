**Double Fine founder Tim Schafer has opened up about the studio's recent move to independence, the shifting landscape at Xbox, and his hopes for the future of the games industry.** The creator behind Day of the Tentacle and Psychonauts recently discussed these topics in a wide-ranging interview, giving fans insight into why Double Fine and Microsoft parted ways and what comes next for the San Francisco-based team.

## Why Double Fine Left Xbox

Schafer pointed out that **Double Fine’s original mission under Xbox was to support Game Pass with a diverse lineup of smaller IPs**, contributing both back-catalogue and new games to the subscription service. However, he noted that Microsoft's strategy changed: "They're going in a different direction with fewer, larger IPs. So our technique of making a lot of little IPs isn't aligned with that. We wanted to keep doing that, so we went our separate ways." Despite these changes, Schafer remains a supporter of Game Pass, believing the service helped lower barriers for players to discover Double Fine’s quirky titles. He praised Xbox for allowing Double Fine to fully express itself and said he had no regrets about their partnership.

## Facing Industry Uncertainty

Schafer also discussed the larger challenges facing game developers today, particularly the ongoing wave of layoffs. He admitted, "I don't understand the economics of making games," and noted that the industry has remained in a downturn for years despite an active player base. While expressing uncertainty about the long-term causes, Schafer suspects that greed may play a role and hopes that the situation is just part of a cycle that will eventually rebound. He remains optimistic, pointing to the "life, excitement, and creativity" thriving in the indie scene and praising creative efforts on both the indie and triple-A sides of the industry, even as the environment becomes more difficult for developers.

## What’s Next for Double Fine

Currently, Double Fine is in the midst of its Amnesia Fortnite game jam, a tradition where the team prototypes four smaller-scope games. This year’s event saw the public voting on these new projects, which include a **city builder with buildings that transform into giant robots and a co-op dungeon-crawler called Bard Crawl**.
