**Nvidia’s next-generation GPU lineup could look very different than expected, with mounting rumors that the flagship RTX 5090 may be dropped and a new high-memory RTX 5080 could be on the horizon.** According to recent industry chatter, the **RTX 5090 is rumored to be axed**, as Nvidia reportedly redirects its GB202 chips toward the professional RTX Pro lineup instead of the gaming-focused GeForce cards. Amid these changes, insiders suggest a 24GB variant of the RTX 5080 could debut as a replacement—potentially arriving as early as the first quarter of 2027.

## The shifting GPU landscape

These developments come from reports indicating that Nvidia has **verbally notified add-in board partners** like Asus and MSI about the possible arrival of a 24GB RTX 5080, though concrete production details appear to be scarce. This version matches the VRAM previously rumored for an RTX 5080 Super, so hardware manufacturers may already be gearing up for a future launch timeline. Still, as with many Nvidia rumors, plans can change—and the 50-series refresh remains far from official.

Importantly, the hypothetical **24GB RTX 5080** wouldn't offer an increase in core count or significant performance leap for gaming, as it would be based on the already-maxed-out GB203 chip. Instead, the sizable VRAM targets users interested in local AI workloads or other memory-intensive tasks—filling a gap for those who want more power than standard gaming GPUs without leaping to costly professional cards.

## What to expect for PC gamers

If these rumors hold true, **the only gaming-focused Nvidia GPU in 2027 could be the RTX 5070 Ti—potentially leaving enthusiasts with fewer high-end options and elevated prices**. As the high-performance GB202 silicon shifts toward pro cards, the new 24GB RTX 5080 may become the go-to option for power users looking beyond pure gaming performance. However, these details have not been confirmed by Nvidia, and the company’s plans could change before any official announcements.
