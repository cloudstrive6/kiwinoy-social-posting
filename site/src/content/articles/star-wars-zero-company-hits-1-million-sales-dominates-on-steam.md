Star Wars Zero Company has made a strong debut, with **estimated sales reaching 1 million copies** since its launch. Developed by Bit Reactor—starring veterans from XCOM's Firaxis team—this Star Wars strategy game is proving popular, particularly on PC, where **about 69.9% of sales are from Steam**. The rest is split with **19.9% on PlayStation** and **10.2% on Xbox/Xbox PC**. Industry analyst Rhys Elliott suggests the title has generated around $50 million so far, a **"respectable" performance in a competitive period for the genre**.

## How Zero Company Is Performing
While these numbers would be an achievement for most strategy games, there's uncertainty surrounding whether they meet the expectations of publisher EA. The company has not released official sales figures or commented on Zero Company’s commercial performance, and now that EA is private, no updates on revenue are expected soon. Meanwhile, the game's studio, Bit Reactor, reportedly furloughed **about 80% of its staff before launch** after running out of funds, with only a skeleton crew left to handle post-launch issues. Bit Reactor described this as a tough but necessary call that was not influenced by EA or Disney.

## The Big Picture: Sales Trends and Regional Gaps
Despite its Star Wars pedigree, Zero Company’s popularity largely stays in the West—**90% of its playerbase are in North America or Europe**, with less than 5% in Asia. On Steam, global reviews are mostly positive (**82% positive on average**), but response from Chinese players is less enthusiastic, citing missing voiceover and localisation features. This limited appeal in Asian markets is a recurring challenge for Star Wars games, and the lack of proper localisation may have compounded the problem for Zero Company.

The last major patch for the game landed on September 10, but communications from Bit Reactor have since gone quiet. Still, critics have praised the game: IGN gave Zero Company a **9/10**, commending its cinematic presentation and its story focused on non-Force users.
