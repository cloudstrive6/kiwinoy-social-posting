**Several exhibitors at Gamescom have suffered thefts of laptops and essential demo equipment from their booths in Cologne, leaving indie developers and studios scrambling to salvage their show presence.** The reported incidents have forced some creators to end their Gamescom appearances early, while others are now facing the risk of leaked builds and lost data. Organizers and affected companies have since addressed the situation, highlighting both the emotional toll and the broader security implications for the event. 

## Booths Raided Overnight
One of the most affected exhibitors was **indie developer Ryan Laley**, who discovered that his booth for the asymmetric survival horror game Mimic had been broken into overnight. Laley described the experience as “heartbreaking,” noting that as a solo developer, the loss of equipment is both devastating and expensive. Laptops were the main targets, with thieves leaving chargers behind but making off with the devices themselves. Police reports were filed, though hopes for recovery are low. 

Laley’s experience was echoed by **other exhibitors**, including iam8bit, which reported that two of its demo laptops were stolen from the business area despite onsite security. These devices contained playable builds of games like Petal Runner and Escape Academy 2, raising fears of potential leaks. Tessera Studios, developers of The Zibbo Show, similarly reported the loss of laptops and a Steam Deck containing both personal and critical company data. Several other booths, including Games from Spain and Ukiyo Studios, also reported thefts, some noting previous experiences and heightened security measures at Gamescom compared to other events.

## Security Under Scrutiny
In response, **Gamescom organizers have acknowledged the thefts** and stated that uniformed guards provide general security across the venue, while individual booth security can be arranged by exhibitors separately. All reported cases have been forwarded to both Gamescom management and local police, with investigations reportedly underway. Organizers emphasized that insurance for valuable equipment is encouraged in their guidelines and that such incidents are considered individual cases, not the norm.
