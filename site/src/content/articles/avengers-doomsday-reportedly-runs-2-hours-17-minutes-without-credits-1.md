**Avengers: Doomsday** may be a leaner film than many fans expected. A new, unconfirmed report from scooper @Cryptic4KQual claims the movie runs just **2 hours, 17 minutes without credits**, which is **13 minutes shorter** than the official runtime listed by Marvel Studios and Disney.

Marvel Studios has officially listed the **December 18** blockbuster at **2 hours, 30 minutes**, a figure co-director **Joe Russo** corroborated while speaking at the Qatar Film Committee's Doha Industry Days. The film is the studio's biggest crossover yet, featuring characters from across the multiverse, so many fans anticipated a far longer movie. The length has been a major talking point since **Robert Downey Jr.** was unveiled as Doctor Doom and Marvel pivoted away from Avengers: The Kang Dynasty.

## Why a shorter runtime may not matter

The reported length mirrors **Avengers: Infinity War**, which ran 2 hours, 29 minutes and the same 2 hours, 17 minutes without credits. Like Infinity War setting up Avengers: Endgame, Doomsday is likely to act as a setup for **Avengers: Secret Wars**, arriving **December 17, 2027**. Joe Russo has said the "story is so large that it really needed to be a two-parter," and the second part should run much longer, much like Endgame's three-hour length.

The Infinity Saga arguably did a better job establishing Thanos than Marvel has with Doom. However, the Russos have compensated with extra footage in **Avengers Endgame: Encore**, the re-release of the Infinity Saga capper. VisionQuest also included a post-credits scene setting up the return of Elizabeth Olsen's Scarlet Witch, and reports suggest **Spider-Man: Brand New Day**'s extended cut, arriving **December 4**, will include extra Doomsday footage.

## What to expect from the story

Doomsday is set to show the effects of incursions and what it looks like when the multiverse completely falls apart. It will explore how past choices by various heroes set the collapse in motion, most notably Steve Rogers' decision to live a full life in another timeline with Peggy Carter. Doom's vendetta is personal; the Russos have already revealed he lost a child. His crusade will hunt heroes across multiple timelines and force strangers into an alliance.

In the trailers, the Avengers and other hero groups look disjointed, which may be intentional. A major loss against Doom, backed by the Sentinels, the Scarlet Witch and the Hulk among others, would be a strong springboard for Secret Wars, with the heroes regrouping into a more cohesive unit.
